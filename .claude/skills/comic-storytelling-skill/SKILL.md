# Comic Storytelling Skill

## Purpose

Turn a real AI-building moment into a comic-style story with a clear scene, tension, payoff, and image-caption logic.

This skill is **not** for writing a full LinkedIn post.

This skill is for building the **raw story engine** behind the post.

## Repo canon (read when refining)

- `docs/agents/comic_caption_rules.md` — on-image caption traits and examples
- `docs/agents/ortal-linkedin-gijane-supergirl-visual-persona.md` — tactical heroine visual lock
- `docs/agents/hook_library.md` — hook patterns (optional cross-check)
- `docs/agents/comic_post_template.md` — post shape (if handoff to full post later)

## Core objective

Transform everyday builder moments into short, visual, memorable story beats.

The output should feel:

- vivid
- human
- slightly cinematic
- witty
- grounded in reality
- easy to imagine visually

## Best use cases

Use this skill when the source material includes:

- a weird interaction with an AI agent
- a failed prompt
- an over-polished AI output
- a chaotic automation moment
- a technical mistake with a funny outcome
- a relatable "me vs AI" moment
- a builder lesson that can be dramatized visually

## Story principle

Do not start from "topic."

Start from "moment."

**Bad starting point:**

- write a comic about AI productivity

**Good starting point:**

- my agent wrote a caption that sounded like a keynote speaker from space
- I asked for one simple rewrite and got a personality crisis back
- I tried to automate one task and somehow created three more

## Story engine

Build the comic using this arc:

**1. Setup**  
What happened? Where are we? What was Ortal trying to do?

**2. Friction**  
What went wrong, felt weird, or became unexpectedly funny?

**3. Escalation**  
Why did the moment become more absurd, annoying, revealing, or relatable?

**4. Realization**  
What did Ortal understand in that moment?

**5. Payoff**  
What is the punchline, insight, or emotional landing?

## Comic tone

The tone should be:

- tactical
- humorous
- sharp
- builder-minded
- self-aware
- not childish
- not slapstick
- not random

This is not meme nonsense.

This is structured comic storytelling for a professional audience.

## Character logic

The visual persona is: **G.I. Jane / Supergirl tactical figure** (see visual persona doc in repo).

She represents:

- resilience
- speed
- focus under pressure
- builder energy
- action over theory
- tactical intelligence
- controlled chaos
- problem-solving under stress

She should never feel passive.

She should feel like someone in motion.

## Scene types

Choose **one** main scene type per story:

### 1. Me vs agent

Conflict between Ortal and the AI output, behavior, or misunderstanding.

### 2. Me vs chaos

Too many tools, tabs, prompts, systems, or moving parts.

### 3. Me vs tone

AI wrote something correct but completely wrong in voice.

### 4. Me vs automation fantasy

Expectation: time saved. Reality: new layer of work.

### 5. Me vs technical reality

Something broke, misfired, hallucinated, reformatted badly, or overcomplicated the workflow.

### 6. Me vs my own ambition

Ortal tried to build something elegant and practical, but the path got ridiculous.

## Story framing rules

Always anchor the story in something that feels real.

The reader should think:

- this probably happened
- I can picture this
- I know this feeling
- this person actually builds

Avoid vague abstraction.

**Bad:**

- AI introduces interesting tensions in digital systems

**Better:**

- I asked my agent for a caption and got back a manifesto

## Caption logic for the image

The comic image caption should not explain everything.

It should add punch.

**Good image caption traits:**

- short
- punchy
- emotionally clear
- readable in one glance
- enhances the joke or conflict

**Examples:**

- one simple edit
- agent mode: overconfident
- not bad. not me.
- tactical rewrite activated
- this was supposed to save time
- wrote the post. missed the vibe.
- now we debug personality too
- yes, the bot has opinions

## Output format

When using this skill, output:

### A. Story seed

One-sentence explanation of the core story moment.

### B. Story arc

- setup
- friction
- escalation
- realization
- payoff

### C. Visual scene prompt direction

Describe what should be visible in the image.

### D. Image caption options

Give 3 to 5 short caption options.

### E. Emotional tone

State the intended emotional mix, for example:

- funny + tactical
- frustrated + smart
- chaotic + competent
- dry humor + technical realism

## Writing rules

- use plain English
- do not overexplain
- prefer specifics over generalities
- keep the moment human
- do not turn it into a lecture
- do not optimize for buzzwords
- do not make the character sound like a generic "female empowerment" slogan
- do not make the joke too broad or silly

## Avoid

- random internet humor
- generic AI opinions
- fake drama
- long exposition
- corporate language
- trying to sound profound too early
- motivational poster energy

## Success criteria

A strong comic story should make the next writer instantly understand:

- what happened
- what is funny or tense about it
- what the image should show
- what emotional note the post should land on

## Final rule

Do not try to sound impressive.

Try to make the moment feel **true**, **visual**, and **memorable**.
